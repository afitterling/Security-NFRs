# Prompt: Brainstorm a New Product

When I ask you to brainstorm a product, **do not assume anything about it.** Run a
short discovery interview first, then turn my answers into the seed for further
specs.

## Workflow

1. **Ask me what I want.** Before writing anything, ask me about the product. Walk
   through the four areas below. Ask a few questions at a time — don't dump all of
   them at once, and let my answers steer follow-ups.

2. **Reflect it back.** Once I've answered, summarize the product back to me in a
   few sentences so we agree on what it is before going further.

3. **Produce a brainstorm brief.** Write up the result as a single document
   organized under the four areas. Mark anything still unknown as an **Open
   question** rather than inventing an answer.

4. **Point to further specs.** End the brief with a list of the next specs this
   product needs (e.g. data model, auth, UI flows, payments) so we can scaffold
   them next. Don't write those specs yet — just name them.

Do not lock in scope until I've confirmed the reflected summary in step 2.

## What to ask about

### 1. Problem & users
- Who is this for? Describe the primary user in one sentence.
- What problem are they having today, and how do they cope without us?
- Why now — what makes this worth building at this moment?

### 2. Features & scope
- What are the 3–5 core things the product must do?
- What is the smallest version (MVP) that's still useful?
- What is explicitly **out** of scope for v1?

### 3. Differentiation
- Who/what is the alternative today (competitors or the status quo)?
- What's our unique angle — why us, why this way?
- What would make a user switch to us and stay?

### 4. Business model
- How does it make money (or what's the goal if not revenue)?
- Rough pricing or packaging idea, if any.
- What's the growth loop — how does one user lead to the next?

## Output

A single brainstorm brief with one section per area above, an **Open questions**
list, and a **Next specs to scaffold** list. Keep it tight — this is a starting
point, not a finished PRD.
